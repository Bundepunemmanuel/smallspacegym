Why your pull-up bar might be leaving marks you'll pay for.

Screw-in bars are the most common, the cheapest, and the single worst choice for a rented door frame — four holes you'll be patching at move-out. Tension-mounted bars hold real body weight using pressure and padded end caps instead, and come down in seconds when you're not using them.

What to look for

A tension-mount bar

Confirm it's rated for your actual door frame width — most are adjustable, but cheap ones have a narrower range than advertised.

Padded end caps

Thin or hard caps are what actually scuff paint over repeated use — padding thickness matters more than the bar material itself.

A weight-rated frame

Look for the stated body-weight limit, and give yourself real margin if you plan to add resistance bands to the bar later.

Before you buy

  • Measure both the width and the depth of your door frame's trim — not every bar fits a shallow or oddly shaped frame.
  • Reinstall it in the exact same spot each time to avoid new pressure points on the frame.
  • Remove it when not training for a few weeks straight — frames weren't built to hold constant tension indefinitely.
